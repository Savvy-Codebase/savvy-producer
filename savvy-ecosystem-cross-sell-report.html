01 / Executive recommendation

Give each site one job.

Treat the four brands as a connected path through an agent’s growth—not four copies of one broad sales pitch. Each site should answer one question, make one primary offer, and hand off only when another brand is a better fit.

02 / Do all four sites make sense?

Four brands can make sense.
Four full websites need to earn their keep.

Direct answer: the four offers are different enough to support four brands, but the available material does not yet prove that all four need equally large, independently marketed websites. Separate domains add clarity when each one has a distinct buyer, offer, conversion path, and accountable owner. Without that separation, they split search authority, analytics, maintenance, and trust.

Recommendation: Keep Savvy Leads and Voxr AI standalone: their catalog/checkout and software-demo journeys are distinct. Use Savvy Producer for expertise, education, and broad navigation. Savvy Marketing is the consolidation candidate: retain its service offer, but consider placing it under Savvy Producer until its distinct demand and ownership justify a separate acquisition site.

Use these gates before investing in a separate site

Keep it separate when…

  • Different buyer or job.
  • Distinct offer, fulfillment, and CTA.
  • Own qualified traffic and conversions.
  • Named owner for upkeep and results.

Combine it when…

  • Visitors cannot tell where to go.
  • Offer and buyer duplicate another site.
  • Little unique content, traffic, or sales.
  • Extra domain fragments trust and reporting.
Decision to make now: Decide whether Savvy Marketing is a separate managed-service business. If yes, keep its site with a distinct owner and funnel. If no, move the offer under Savvy Producer and plan URL redirects after checking brand equity, links, and analytics.

This recommendation is based on available materials, not traffic or revenue data. Use a 60–90 day measurement period to confirm the final architecture.

03 / Content architecture

What belongs where

Make the content useful on its home site, then link across only when the visitor’s next need is clear. Keep product details, checkout, and software claims on the site responsible for delivering them.

Savvy Producer · trust & guidance

Teach, diagnose, then direct

Founder expertise and practical agent education; sales-process and agency-growth resources; webinars; case studies with substantiated outcomes; a short “which path fits?” chooser. Primary CTA: Talk through your growth plan. Secondary links: buy leads, get campaign help, or see Voxr.

Savvy Marketing · done-with-you / done-for-you

Sell campaign outcomes and service

Live-transfer and appointment campaigns, past-book and retention campaigns, team webinars, implementation/support, qualification and campaign process, pilot plans, and proof. Primary CTA: Plan a campaign. Link to Savvy Leads for self-serve inventory; Voxr for software-led follow-up.

Savvy Leads · catalog & transaction

Help buyers choose and order

Lead types and availability, territory map/reservation, delivery method, filters, pricing, sample records, consent and provenance information, replacement/refund terms, and ordering. Primary CTA: Check availability / order. Cross-sell a follow-up workflow only after the buyer understands the lead product.

Voxr AI · software & proof

Show the workflow, not just AI

Product demos, use cases, integrations, onboarding, security/compliance explanations, transparent pricing or evaluation path, customer proof, and human escalation controls. Primary CTA: See a demo. Link to Savvy Leads for inventory and Savvy Marketing for campaign implementation.

Shared ecosystem standards

  • Use one discreet “Savvy ecosystem” menu or footer with one-sentence descriptions and direct links; never bounce visitors through a generic hub by default.
  • Use consistent logo, voice, support expectations, privacy disclosures, and naming. Explain which entity fulfills each order or software service.
  • Give every page one dominant CTA and at most one contextual next step. Preserve the visitor’s current task and avoid interruptive cross-sells.
  • Put testimonials, performance statements, lead-quality claims, and ROI examples beside evidence and context; obtain permission and substantiate before reuse.

Send intent to the right front door.

Channel priorities

  • Organic: build helpful search pages around product, territory, campaign, and software use cases.
  • Founder/social: practical education and proof → Savvy Producer resource or matching specialist page.
  • Paid search: high-intent keywords → dedicated campaign/product/demo pages.
  • Partner referrals: co-branded, clearly attributed landing pages.
  • Email: consented education and customer lifecycle; keep B2B outbound separate, compliant, and targeted.

Traffic rules

  • Do not send every ad or social post to Savvy Producer’s generic home page.
  • Do not use Savvy Leads as the destination for software or managed-service ads.
  • Use direct cross-links with a plain explanation of why the next site is relevant.
  • Carry source, campaign, landing page, and prior site through the handoff.
  • Optimize to qualified orders, activations, and retained revenue—not raw form fills.
05 / Customer workflow

One journey, with helpful branches.

A visitor should move from need → fitting solution → successful use → relevant next offer. The path should be reversible: keep the visitor’s original goal easy to complete.

1

Discover. Visitor arrives from search, social, referral, event, or paid campaign. Record landing URL, referrer, UTM source/medium/campaign/content, and first-touch attribution.

2

Match intent. Use a short choice or focused landing page: “I need leads,” “I need a campaign managed,” “I need faster follow-up,” or “I’m working on my agency.” Don’t require a long questionnaire before showing value.

3

Route to the right brand. Leads intent → Savvy Leads catalog/order. Managed campaign intent → Savvy Marketing consult/pilot. Automation intent → Voxr demo/evaluation. Broad or unclear need → Savvy Producer guide or strategy call.

4

Capture and qualify. Collect only what is needed to fulfill the request. Preserve first and latest source, need category, product/territory, team size or volume band, requested contact method, and relevant consent evidence.

5

Respond and hand off. Create/route the contact in the shared CRM (GHL, if selected as the system of record); assign a human owner and response SLA. Pass source and context across site/domain boundaries; do not make the visitor repeat details.

6

Convert and onboard. Complete order, consultation, or demo. Explain fulfillment, expectations, ownership, support, and next steps. Track activation, not just form completion.

7

Offer the next useful thing. After a Savvy Leads order, ask whether follow-up is the bottleneck → Voxr demo if yes. For a buyer needing campaign management → Savvy Marketing. For a Voxr customer short on inventory → Savvy Leads. For any customer needing broader sales/process guidance → Savvy Producer.

Example: An agent finds a Savvy Producer guide on improving lead response, then chooses a Savvy Leads product. After the first order, a service check asks whether the agent can contact leads quickly. If not, offer a Voxr workflow demo. If the agent needs a team to run the campaign, introduce Savvy Marketing. Each handoff is tied to a stated need, not an automatic bundle.

06 / Measurement & rollout

Measure the ecosystem as one business.

Keep brand-level reporting for accountability, then connect it to the customer journey so the team can see which first touch produces qualified customers and which next offer actually helps them.

Shared attribution fields

  • First-touch source and date; latest-touch source and date.
  • UTM values, referrer, entry page, campaign, and sending brand.
  • Intent category, product interest, vertical, territory, and volume band.
  • Consent/source evidence and requested contact method.
  • Qualified, booked, ordered, activated, repeat purchase, and attached service/product.

Core scorecard

  • Qualified conversion by source and landing page.
  • Lead/order and demo-to-paid conversion.
  • Response time, booking/show rate, and activation.
  • Repeat order, cross-sell attach rate, and 30/60/90-day retention.
  • Revenue and gross margin per customer/cohort and by acquisition source.

HighLevel’s documentation distinguishes first and latest attribution and describes attribution capture on supported forms, calendars, chat, and order forms. Use this as a practical starting point, then validate cross-domain tracking and data persistence in the actual GHL setup. Source: HighLevel attribution guide.

Guardrails before launch

  • Confirm the actual offer, price, inventory, delivery, and support promise on every destination.
  • Review lead consent and provenance, privacy disclosures, suppression, and any phone/SMS/AI voice workflows with qualified counsel.
  • The FCC states that TCPA restrictions on artificial or prerecorded voice include current AI-generated human voices. Do not assume an AI voice campaign is exempt; review consent and applicable rules before use.
  • For commercial email, the FTC says CAN-SPAM applies to B2B email too; maintain accurate sender details, a valid postal address, opt-out handling, and required disclosures.
